# Referee decisions — SME-119

Fixtures now resolve foul evidence, advantage, cards and free/penalty kicks. The implementation follows IFAB 2026/27 Laws 12–14. It builds on the fixture and offside systems without changing practice-only worlds.

## Challenges and sanctions

The existing tackle draw still determines the displayed chance of winning the ball. A deterministic partition of its failed outcome distinguishes a clean miss, careless contact, reckless contact and excessive force. Distance and fatigue affect that partition; the referee never consumes an extra random draw. Both player and AI tackles use the same path. Failed close goalkeeper rushes can also produce contact offences. This is a prototype contact model, not physical collision or referee vision simulation.

`FoulRules` separates evidence from consequences. Careless challenges normally carry no card; reckless challenges are cautioned; excessive force leads to dismissal. Position determines a direct free kick or penalty. The model considers direction towards goal, control, distance and covering defenders when judging a denied obvious opportunity or stopped promising attack. An attempt to play the ball can mitigate the sanction when a penalty is awarded; deliberate handball does not receive that mitigation. Pure rule evidence also covers holding, dangerous play and non-contact obstruction. Those latter gestures are not independently generated by the current capsule animations.

## Advantage

When a player retains possession after a suitable challenge, the referee allows up to three match seconds to realise a benefit. Six metres of forward progress or committing a shot consumes the advantage; losing control, an intervening stoppage/offside, or expiry recalls the original foul location. Thinking and inspection never spend this window. This bounded model uses actual match ticks, not wall-clock time.

A realised advantage defers any required reckless caution until the next stoppage, including halftime/full time. A caution based only on stopping a promising attack is removed when advantage succeeds. Sending-off and second-yellow offences stop immediately; the prototype does not exercise the referee's exceptional discretion to continue a clear scoring opportunity after serious misconduct. An unresolved earlier advantage takes priority over another challenge in the callback window.

## Dismissals

The discipline ledger applies each incident once, counts yellows, and turns the second caution into a dismissal. Sent-off players are removed from the active roster, targets, movement and pitch markers. The ledger persists after halftime and exposes `CanParticipate` for future substitution logic. There is currently no substitution/reinsertion route.

If the goalkeeper is dismissed, an existing teammate assumes the match goalkeeper role using their own attributes; no additional player enters. The user's career position is unchanged. A dismissed user may inspect and watch the remaining fixture. Fewer than seven players ends the match as abandoned rather than leaving an invalid simulation running.

## Restarts and keeper offences

Direct and indirect free kicks use legal restart spacing. Direct kicks can be passed or shot; indirect kicks retain the existing direct-goal restrictions. Penalties place the taker at the mark, the goalkeeper on the goal line, and all other players behind the mark, outside the area and arc. The basic penalty is a placed shot through the existing accuracy and separate keeper-save resolvers. Setup and flight may complete after the nominal end of a half. More detailed set-piece roles, targeting and routines belong to SME-120.

Keepers retain their legal choices but can explicitly select **Handle (FOUL)** when a back-pass, teammate throw, own release, or being outside their area makes handling illegal. The label warns that a sanction follows; no dexterity is involved. Inside-area handling restrictions award an indirect kick without a card; outside handling is a direct-kick offence. The engine also tracks the eight match-second hold limit, shows the final five-second countdown, and awards a corner on expiry. Current keeper AI normally releases earlier, and paused decisions do not run this clock.

Referee notices explain the offence, card and restart and freeze the match until Enter/Continue. Inspection remains available. Advantage appears as a non-pausing banner. Player inspection shows a yellow card or sent-off status.

The implemented edition is shown in the referee notice. Simulation thresholds described above are tuning choices, not claims that the Laws prescribe numerical thresholds for judgement.
